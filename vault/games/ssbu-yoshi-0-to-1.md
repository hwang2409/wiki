# SSBU Yoshi 0 -> 1 Scrapbook

Loose page for turning casual Yoshi into deliberate Yoshi. Not a combo encyclopedia, not a matchup chart, not a sermon. Just the scraps that are worth keeping near the controller: states, habits, hitboxes, drills, videos, and places to look when something feels confusing.

![Yoshi render from SmashWiki](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-render.png)

> Current thesis: Yoshi wins when movement makes the other player choose first. Eggs ask for a route. Drift catches the route. Double jump keeps the whole page from falling apart.

## Prereq Vocabulary

Read these once before the rest of the page. No need to memorize everything; these are the labels that make the later notes less annoying.

- **Neutral:** nobody has a clear advantage yet. Both players are trying to win the next interaction.
- **Advantage:** the good part after a hit, knockdown, ledge trap, juggle, or offstage send. The goal is to keep the turn going.
- **Disadvantage:** the bad part after getting hit, cornered, juggled, or forced to recover. The goal is to escape without making the next mistake obvious.
- **Out of shield / OOS:** an action done right after blocking something. `Nair OOS` means shield, then neutral air as the punish.
- **Startup:** how many frames before a move can hit. Lower number = faster button.
- **Landing lag:** recovery time after landing during/after an aerial. High landing lag = easier to punish.
- **Whiff punish:** making someone miss, then hitting them before they recover.
- **Safe on shield:** pressure that is hard to punish after the opponent blocks it. Usually depends on spacing, drift, and landing timing.
- **Drift:** steering in the air. Yoshi lives here.
- **Short hop / fast fall:** small jump / faster descent. The basic way aerial pressure stops feeling floaty.
- **DI:** directional influence. Holding a direction after getting hit to change launch angle and survival/combo routes.
- **Conditioning:** showing one option enough that the opponent starts expecting it, then using the answer to that expectation.
- **Ledge trap:** covering options while the opponent is hanging at ledge.
- **Edgeguard:** leaving stage or threatening offstage to interrupt recovery.

## Baseline Markers

Stuff that should eventually feel normal:

- [ ] The current state has a name: neutral, advantage, disadvantage, ledge, offstage.
- [ ] Eggs are thrown to cover a lane, not to fill silence.
- [ ] Forward air is a punish or callout, not the default way to enter.
- [ ] Nair out of shield happens on purpose.
- [ ] Double jump is saved unless it solves a real problem.
- [ ] Lost stocks have a short explanation after the set.

## Character Thesis

> Air-mobility character. Strong aerials. Fast defensive buttons. Scary edgeguards. Double jump is both armor and lifeline.

The Yoshi shape is not "run in and swing." It is closer to:

1. Hover outside the other character's best range.
2. Put an egg where they want to go.
3. Drift in, drift out, or land beside shield.
4. If they panic, hit them.
5. If they hold shield, grab them.
6. If they are above, at ledge, or offstage, keep the turn.

Autopilot that needs a red circle around it:

- random fair in neutral
- panic nair every time pressure appears
- instant double jump out of disadvantage
- landing the same way three times in a row
- giving up advantage after one hit

## Match-State Notes

- **Neutral:** nobody owns the turn yet. Space is the whole argument. Egg lanes, dash back, drift-back bair, empty land grab, short-hop pressure that can fade away.
- **Advantage:** they are above, cornered, at ledge, or offstage. The page should get smaller for them. Stay under them, check drift with up air, make recovery routes worse with eggs.
- **Disadvantage:** Yoshi is being juggled, cornered, or forced to recover. Drift first, button second. Save double jump. Use platforms. Stop landing with the same scared nair.
- **Ledge:** someone has fewer options than usual. Egg near ledge, wait for jump, punish neutral getup, respect invincible reversals.
- **Offstage:** recovery routes are being negotiated. Egg the path, nair the body, fair the predictable jump, keep double jump unless the stock depends on it.

## Move Sheet

Live reference: [Ultimate Frame Data: Yoshi](https://ultimateframedata.com/yoshi).

- **Neutral air:** fast interrupt, out-of-shield punish, combo break, edgeguard tap. `Frame 3`, `OOS 6`, `7 landing lag`.
- **Up air:** juggle, anti-air, high-percent kill. `Frame 5`, `8 landing lag`.
- **Back air:** safer pressure, damage, drift-back wall. `Frame 11`, multihit, `11 landing lag`.
- **Forward air:** callout, punish, spike, offstage threat. `Frame 16`, `12 landing lag`.
- **Down air:** damage pile, shield pressure, landing mix. Multihit, `17 landing lag`.
- **Egg Throw:** lane control, ledge pressure, anti-camping, recovery harassment. `Frame 16`, explodes later if it misses.
- **Grab:** shield answer. Standing grab `frame 14`, shield grab `18`.

## Hitbox Clippings

### Neutral Air

![Yoshi neutral air hitbox GIF from Ultimate Frame Data](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-nair.gif)

Fast button. Frame 3. The emergency brake.

Good notes:

- out-of-shield when someone lands unsafely
- combo breaker when there is a real gap
- offstage tap against recoveries
- landing option only if the landing mix has not gone stale

Bad smell: every scary moment becomes nair. Good sign: the replay shows why nair was the right answer.

### Up Air

![Yoshi up air hitbox GIF from Ultimate Frame Data](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-uair.gif)

Ceiling button. Frame 5. The note is not "hit up air once." The note is "stay under them until they prove they can land."

Little routes:

- up tilt -> up air
- down throw -> up air when percent and DI allow it
- egg hit -> chase -> up air
- landing read -> up air, then keep tracking drift

Watch for opponents burning jump early. That is usually the next up air.

### Back Air

![Yoshi back air hitbox GIF from Ultimate Frame Data](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-bair.gif)

Wall piece. Multihit. Less dramatic than fair, usually more useful in neutral.

Scraps:

- short hop bair while drifting away
- bair on shield -> leave before the punish
- bair hit -> chase with up air or another aerial
- bair as the "no, you do not get this lane" button

The nice version makes people swing at the space Yoshi just left.

### Forward Air

![Yoshi forward air hitbox GIF from Ultimate Frame Data](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-fair.gif)

Spike / punish / big read. Frame 16. The temptation button.

Use-case pile:

- recovery path is obvious
- opponent keeps jumping into the same airspace
- eggs or safer aerials already made them choose badly
- ledge panic has been conditioned

Neutral fair spam is usually just a diary entry for "I wanted the clip."

### Down Air

![Yoshi down air hitbox GIF from Ultimate Frame Data](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-dair.gif)

Damage shredder. Shield stress. Landing mix. Not free.

Keep it near:

- stuck shields
- hard landing callouts
- platform pressure
- moments where the opponent is frozen and has to respect the multihit

Red flag: dair because landing felt uncomfortable.

### Egg Throw

![Yoshi egg throw image from Ultimate Frame Data](/media/ssbu-yoshi/yoshi-egg-throw.png)

Lane tool. Frame 16. The question each egg asks: jump, shield, wait, or move?

Quick labels:

- high egg: jump check
- low egg: grounded movement check
- ledge egg: getup tax
- recovery egg: route damage
- far egg with no plan: probably noise

## Practice Pile

> Four buckets. One bucket per week. The point is to remove one bad autopilot habit at a time.

### Week 1: Movement / Survival

Keep:

- short-hop fast-fall nair
- short-hop fast-fall bair
- aerial -> drift left / right
- low recovery without instant double jump

Match sticky note:

- no random smash attacks
- no instant double jump from disadvantage
- no automatic jump from ledge

Review tell: did the stock disappear because panic took over?

### Week 2: Neutral

Neutral loop:

1. Stand just outside their good button.
2. Egg a lane.
3. Drift in or drift out.
4. If they shield, grab.
5. If they whiff, punish.
6. If they wait, take space.

Practice crumbs:

- egg angles
- dash back -> whiff punish
- landing nair -> jab or tilt
- spaced bair

Review tell: was the approach chosen, or was boredom driving?

### Week 3: Advantage

Keep the turn after first contact.

- up tilt -> up air
- down throw -> up air
- egg hit -> aerial chase
- ledge egg -> cover jump or neutral getup
- offstage nair/fair edgeguard

Review tell: after winning neutral, did the pressure continue or did everything reset for free?

### Week 4: Disadvantage / Adaptation

Make a little tally during replay review:

- instant double jump
- landing nair
- roll from ledge
- attack from ledge
- always high recovery
- always low recovery

Patch notes for the next set:

- drift away first
- button second
- platforms are exits
- double jump only when the problem is specific

Review tell: what habit did the opponent punish twice?

## Rules / Friction

- Nair is strong. Panic nair is different.
- Fair is a punish, not a personality.
- Eggs control space. They are not background music.
- Double jump is the resource that makes Yoshi weird. Spend it like it matters.
- Shield-heavy opponents are asking to be grabbed.
- If a stock cannot be explained, the replay is the note.
